Impact

This GKN Aerospace site has three tanks in total, and the cooling system in one of these 34,000 gallon tanks became compromised on May 21st. The tank of methyl methacrylate began to overheat and vent vapors, triggering temporary evacuations in Garden Grove, Buena Park, Anaheim, Stanton, Westminster and Cypress. A total of 44,000 people had been told to evacuate by May 23rd. Initially residents and city officials were under the impression that a leak or explosion from the tank was inevitable, however, a crack in the tank allowed pressure to be relieved by May 25th, minimizing the threat of an explosion and allowing the evacuation zone to be reduced.

Conflicts

The tank contained methyl methacrylate, a toxic, highly flammable chemical used to manufacture acrylic plastics, including aircraft canopies and windshields. Methyl methacrylate has low acute (short-term) and chronic (long-term) toxicity to aquatic life. Methyl methacrylate can contribute to the formation of photochemical smog when it reacts with other volatile substances in the air. Consumers can be exposed to methyl methacrylate by exposure to air from production and processing facilities using methyl methacrylate. Since the tank was at risk of leaking, residents of Garden Grove and surrounding neighborhoods could have been exposed, prompting the mass evacuations.

Former GKN Aerospace employee, Valerie Morales, says most California residents are unaware that federal and state law already gives communities the right to know what hazardous materials are stored in nearby facilities, they just don't know to ask. With 46.2% of Garden Grove’s population being foreign born, it can be reasonable to assume that the majority of residents are not aware of these rights, and it is a trend for chemical plants to exist in communities that consist of people of color.
